Questions to Ask a Water Damage Company in Stoughton, MA

    24/7 emergency line(508) 286-8971Tap to call a dispatcher

    By the Stoughton Water Damage Pros team. Published November 14, 2026. Updated November 14, 2026.

    When water is in your home, you may have minutes to choose who to call. These questions help you compare companies quickly, whether you call from a flooded kitchen or from the road. A good company answers them plainly.

    None of this replaces checking your insurance policy, which may have its own requirements.

    Ask how the work will be done

    Ask what steps they will take, in what order, and how long drying is expected to take. You should hear about stopping the source, extraction, drying equipment and daily moisture readings. If the answer is vague, ask again.

    Ask how pricing works

    A responsible company explains how price is set before work starts. Be careful with an exact number given over the phone without knowing the loss. Ask what changes the price, what is included and whether equipment days are counted. Get the scope in writing.

    Ask about documentation and registration

    Ask whether you will receive photos and moisture readings, since insurers often want them. In Massachusetts, home improvement work on an owner-occupied one to four unit home requires a registered contractor, so ask for the registration number. There is no state license for mold remediation, so ask for references.

    Ask who does the work

    Ask whether the crew who arrives are employees or subcontractors, what equipment they bring and how you reach them during the job. Ask what happens if more damage is found.

    Red flags during the first phone call

    Be cautious if a company pressures you to sign right away, will not explain steps, refuses to put a scope in writing or claims it can quote the whole job over the phone. Another red flag is a company that will not say who will come, how they will contact you or what the hours of work will be.

    A good company is comfortable with questions, because it expects them.

    What to have ready when you call

    Have your address, a short description of the source, the time it started, whether the water is still running and whether anyone has been hurt. Have your insurance information handy. This helps the dispatcher decide what equipment to send and what to say about timing.

    • Address and best phone number
    • Source and when it started
    • Whether water is still running
    • Your insurer and policy number

    Questions about timing and availability

    Ask how soon someone can look at the loss and what that means in practice. Be wary of promises of an exact arrival time during a storm or a freeze, because many calls come at once. A realistic company tells you what it knows and updates you if things change.

    Ask whether the same crew lead will manage the job and how to reach them after hours.

    After the work is done

    Ask for the final moisture readings, the photos and a list of what was removed. Ask whether any warranty or follow-up check is offered, and what to watch for in the next few weeks. Keep everything with your insurance records.

    If something smells or looks wrong later, call the company first and document the problem.

    Comparing two companies on the same loss

    Ask both companies the same list of questions, in the same order, and write down the answers. Note who explains the steps clearly, who gives written scopes and who mentions documentation without being asked. Price matters, but so does clarity.

    If two answers sound equally good, choose the company that can start sooner without overpromising.

    Why local presence matters

    A company with a real local address and crews nearby can usually respond faster and knows the area's water patterns. Look for a street address you can verify, a phone number that gets answered and service pages that describe how the work is done. Reviews are helpful only when they come from real customers on platforms you can check.

    What a good first conversation sounds like

    A good dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, whether it is still running, how much there is and whether anyone is in danger. They tell you what to do right now and explain what happens next. They do not rush you into a contract before they know the facts.

    Standards and Sources Behind This Page

    The questions here follow Massachusetts rules for home improvement contractors, which require registration with the Office of Consumer Affairs and Business Regulation for work on existing owner-occupied one to four unit homes. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration is a good reference for what a drying plan should contain.
