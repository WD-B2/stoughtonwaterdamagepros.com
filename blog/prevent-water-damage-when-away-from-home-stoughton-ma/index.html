Prevent Water Damage When You Are Away From Home in Stoughton

    24/7 emergency line(508) 286-8971Tap to call a dispatcher

    By the Stoughton Water Damage Pros team. Published November 14, 2026. Updated November 14, 2026.

    A burst pipe in a home that is empty can run for days before anyone notices. Massachusetts insurance guidance also notes that burst pipe coverage can depend on the home being properly heated and not left unoccupied, so preparing the house matters twice over.

    This checklist covers the steps that make the biggest difference, whether you are leaving for a weekend or a winter.

    Know where the main shutoff is

    Find the main water shutoff and make sure everyone in the house can operate it. For a long trip, shutting off the water at the main and opening a faucet to drain the lines removes most of the risk. Shut off the water heater first or set it to vacation mode.

    Keep the heat on in winter

    Set the thermostat no lower than a level your plumber or insurer recommends, and open cabinet doors on exterior walls so warm air reaches pipes. Insulate pipes in garages, attics and crawl spaces. Heat left too low can void coverage if a pipe bursts.

    Check appliances and drains

    Turn off the washing machine supply valves, check dishwasher and refrigerator lines and clean gutters so water drains away. If you have a sump pump, test it and consider a battery backup. Heavy rain while you are away can push groundwater into a basement.

    Ask someone to look in

    Ask a neighbor or relative to check the house every few days. A smart water sensor near the water heater and washer can send an alert to your phone. These small steps cost little compared with a flooded home.

    A pre-departure walkthrough

    Walk through the house with a short list. Check under sinks, behind the washer and around the water heater for any dampness. Look at the ceiling in rooms below bathrooms. Test the sump pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. Make sure the thermostat is set and the furnace is working.

    Take a quick set of photos for your own record, which also helps if you ever make a claim.

    Who should have a key and the shutoff plan

    Give a trusted person a key and a one-page note that shows the main shutoff, the breaker panel and the number of your plumber. Tell them what to do first, which is close the water, and who to call next. A written note beats a phone call when someone is standing in a wet kitchen.

    Seasonal tweaks to the checklist

    In winter, focus on heat, pipe insulation and the main shutoff. In spring, check gutters, grading and the sump pump before the rains. In summer, check air conditioner drain lines, which can clog and overflow while you are away. In fall, clear leaves, disconnect hoses and close outdoor spigots.

    A quick seasonal pass takes about an hour and covers most of the risk.

    Smart sensors and shutoff valves

    Water sensors cost little and can send phone alerts when they get wet. Automatic shutoff valves close the main when a leak is detected, and a plumber can install one. They add cost, but they can limit a burst to minutes instead of days.

    Even with a sensor, tell a neighbor so someone can act if the alert comes while you are traveling.

    If you come home to water

    Do not rush in. Check for standing water near outlets and the panel before touching anything. Shut off the main if you can reach it safely, and call for help. Photograph what you see before cleanup.

    Call your insurer to report the loss, and call a crew to start extraction. Time matters more after an empty house, since water may have run for days.

    Plans for long absences and seasonal homes

    For a stay of several weeks, consider draining the system with help from a plumber, setting the thermostat to a safe minimum and arranging weekly checks. For a seasonal home, have the plumber winterize the lines and record the steps so they can be reversed in spring.

    Tell your insurer about long vacancies, because some policies limit coverage when a home is unoccupied.

    Standards and Sources Behind This Page

    The shutoff and heat advice here follows guidance from the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, which says burst pipe coverage can depend on the home being properly heated and not left unoccupied. Pipe insulation and sump pump testing are standard recommendations from FEMA and the American Red Cross.
