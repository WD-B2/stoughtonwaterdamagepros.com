Emergency Flood Damage Cleanup in Stoughton, MA

When rain, river overflow or a drain failure floods a Stoughton property, we remove the water, clean affected rooms and dry the structure.

  • Basements, garages and first floors
  • Contamination awareness

Flood damage restoration in Stoughton means more than pumping a basement. Floodwater carries soil, bacteria and debris, and it soaks into materials differently than a clean pipe leak. Call our 24/7 line before you re-enter a flooded room. Stay out of standing water near electrical outlets, leave the power off at the panel if water is above outlets, and do not run a household vacuum on wet floors.

Our crew handles pump-out, cleanup and drying as one connected job.

Floodwater is not clean water

Water that came from outside, a storm drain or a backed up sewer line should be treated as contaminated until proven otherwise. Porous items that touched it, like carpet, pad and insulation, usually cannot be saved.

Protect yourself first:

  • Wear boots and gloves if you must enter
  • Keep children and pets out of the affected rooms
  • Turn off gas and electricity if water is rising near them
  • Photograph the water line on walls before anything is moved

Flood patterns in Stoughton

The local hazard mitigation plan identifies repeated flooding at the Red Wing Brook area, Pratts Court, the West Street culvert at Ames Long Pond, a storm drain behind the Police Station and along Steep Hill Brook, which drains about 5.3 square miles. About 1,389 acres of town fall in a FEMA special flood hazard area.

Past events include flooding on March 29, 2010 that produced a federal declaration, and a June 28, 2020 flash flood with reports on Pearl Street.

What flood damage looks like once water recedes

Receding water hides a lot.

Waterlines on walls

A line shows how high water reached and how much drywall is affected.

Silt or residue on floors

Fine sediment is a sign of floodwater instead of a plumbing leak.

Swollen trim and doors

Wood absorbs water and swells within a day.

Warped flooring

Cupping or lifting means the subfloor is still wet.

The restoration sequence

We work from the bottom up so water does not travel back into cleaned areas.

  1. Pump out

    Submersible pumps lower the water level, and extractors take over for the last inch.

  2. Remove unsalvageable materials

    Wet carpet, pad and soaked drywall are bagged and removed.

  3. Clean and disinfect

    Surfaces that can be saved are cleaned with appropriate products.

  4. Dry and verify

    Equipment runs until meter readings reach normal levels.

Salvage or discard

Floodwater changes the rules on what is worth saving.

ItemUsual outcomeNote
Carpet and padDiscardContaminated water cannot be reliably cleaned from pad
Drywall wet above the waterlineOften removedWicking carries moisture upward
Solid wood furnitureOften cleanableDepends on how long it sat wet
Upholstered itemsDiscard if floodwaterFabric absorbs contaminants

For pure groundwater or rain in a lower level, see basement water damage cleanup. If the source was a sewer line, use sewage backup cleanup.

What decides the cost of flood work

The depth and duration of the flood, the number of rooms, the amount of material removed and the days of drying equipment all shape the price. You get the scope and the price before work starts.

On insurance:

  • A standard Massachusetts homeowners policy does not cover flood damage and offers no endorsement for it
  • Flood insurance is a separate policy, and many policies have a waiting period
  • Sewer backup and sump pump failure are also excluded unless you added an endorsement

Check your declarations page now so you know what you carry before filing.
