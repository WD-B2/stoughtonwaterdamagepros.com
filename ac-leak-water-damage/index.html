How air conditioners leak

Cooling coils pull moisture from the air and send it down a drain line. When algae clogs the line or the pan rusts through, water backs up and overflows. Attic air handlers are the worst place for this, because the water lands directly on ceiling drywall below.

Frozen coils, low refrigerant and dirty filters can also cause overflow when the ice melts.

What an AC leak looks like

Most signs show up near the air handler.

Stains below the unit

Brown rings on the ceiling beneath an attic air handler are the classic sign.

Standing water in the pan

A pan that stays full means the drain is clogged.

Musty odor from vents

Damp insulation and moist ducts produce odors.

Water near the outdoor unit

Pooling at the condenser pad can point to drainage trouble.

How we dry an AC leak

The work focuses on insulation, drywall and framing.

  1. Shut off and trace

    The system is turned off and the water path is traced.

  2. Remove wet material

    Soaked insulation and saturated drywall are removed.

  3. Dry the cavity

    Dehumidifiers and air movers run until readings are normal.

  4. Recheck for mold risk

    Surfaces are inspected before closing up so damp spots do not stay hidden.

Mistakes That Make AC Leaks Worse

Running the system until the stain appears is the main one. Pouring bleach down the condensate line is another, since it can corrode fittings. Skipping the filter change is a third, because a clogged filter can freeze the coil and overload the pan when it melts.

Ask your HVAC technician what caused the overflow, and keep the note for your insurer.

What to Do Before We Arrive

These steps limit damage while you wait for a visit.

  • Turn the air conditioner off at the thermostat
  • Place a bucket under any drip and photograph the stain
  • Move furniture and rugs out from under wet ceilings
  • Do not press on a sagging ceiling or poke at it

Standards and Sources Behind This Page

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration classifies water by contamination category and by how much a material absorbs. Condensate water is generally treated as clean at the start, but it can pick up contaminants from dirty pans and coils, so drying should begin quickly.
