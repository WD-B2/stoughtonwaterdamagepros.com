Kitchen water damage restoration in Stoughton often involves several materials at once, including cabinets, flooring, drywall and sometimes the ceiling below. Call us to plan a visit. Shut off the supply valve under the sink, empty the cabinet base, and photograph the damage before cleanup.

Kitchens are crowded with pipes and appliances, so one small leak can touch several systems.

Where kitchen leaks start

Under-sink supply lines, dishwasher connections, refrigerator ice maker lines and slow faucet drips are the most common. Dishwasher leaks are especially sneaky, since they run behind the toe kick and soak the subfloor.

Cabinets made of particleboard swell and deteriorate quickly once wet.

What to look for

Kitchens show wet damage in a few places.

Swollen cabinet base

Particleboard bottoms bulge when wet.

Floor soft near the sink

Subfloor may be saturated.

Musty smell under the sink

Damp cabinets smell stale.

Water stains on the ceiling below

A second floor kitchen leak shows below.

How we dry a kitchen

The work targets cabinets, floors and walls together.

  1. Find all wet areas

    Meters map the cabinets, floor and wall.

  2. Remove water

    Extractors clear any standing water.

  3. Open access

    Toe kicks and cabinet backs are opened so air reaches the cavity.

  4. Dry and verify

    Equipment runs until readings are normal.

What a Kitchen Visit Involves

A kitchen assessment begins at the source. The technician checks under the sink, behind the dishwasher and around the refrigerator, then moves outward to the toe kicks, the base of the island and the floor. Readings are taken in each spot and noted on a simple sketch.

Because cabinets are expensive, the crew explains which ones can be dried in place and which are likely to fail. You see the readings, and the plan is explained before anything is removed.

Mistakes That Make Kitchen Leaks Worse

Closing the toe kick again after wiping up is the classic error. It traps water against the cabinet base, where particleboard swells and mold can start. Another is ignoring the ceiling below a second floor kitchen until a stain appears.

A third is replacing the countertop first. Until the cabinet base is dry, a new counter simply sits on a wet box.

  • Open the toe kick to let air reach the base
  • Check the room under the kitchen for stains
  • Keep appliance receipts and photos of the leak for your insurer

After the Kitchen Is Dry

When readings are normal, you can plan repairs. Replace damaged cabinets, seal around the sink and check supply lines. A plumber can add shutoff valves or replace aging hoses. Keep drying records so the contractor knows the cabinet bases are ready.

Why Kitchens Need Careful Sequencing

Kitchens pack plumbing, appliances, cabinets and flooring into a small space, so the order of work matters. A crew that rushes to remove one material before measuring the others can miss a wet subfloor. That hidden moisture later grows mold or cups new flooring.

What to Do Before We Arrive

A few steps protect cabinets and floors while you wait for a visit.

  • Close the supply valve under the sink or behind the appliance
  • Empty the cabinet base and wipe it dry
  • Open the toe kick if you can do so without tools
  • Photograph the damage and the source

Standards and Sources Behind This Page

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration describes how materials such as cabinet boxes, subfloor and drywall should be measured and dried. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance notes that sudden and accidental losses are commonly covered, while gradual leaks often are not.
