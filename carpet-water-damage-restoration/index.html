Carpet is the easy layer. The pad and subfloor beneath it hold the real problem.

Why carpet fails when wet

Carpet fibers dry quickly, but the pad underneath acts like a sponge and stays wet for days. Subfloor beneath can hold water that wicks back into fibers. Moisture trapped under carpet is a common source of mold and odor.

Clean water from a supply leak is the best case. Gray or black water is not.

Signs carpet has water damage

Watch for these.

Squishy feel

Water in the pad squishes underfoot.

Musty odor

Smell shows moisture is staying.

Discoloration at edges

Stains along baseboards show wicking.

Loose or rippled carpet

Moisture can break down the adhesive or stretch the backing.

How we handle wet carpet

Speed is more important than anything else.

  1. Extract

    Weighted extractors pull water from carpet and pad.

  2. Lift or float

    Carpet is lifted so air reaches the pad and subfloor.

  3. Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the layers.

  4. Decide

    Pad is usually replaced, carpet is kept if clean and dry.

What to Expect When the Crew Arrives

The first thing the crew lead does is identify the water type, because that decides everything else. A technician presses a meter against the carpet, then lifts a corner to check the pad and tack strip. You will hear whether the carpet can be kept and why.

If the water was clean and the carpet is a good candidate, the team extracts, lifts and supports the carpet on air so both sides dry. If not, they cut it into manageable strips and remove it with the pad, and you get a written note of what was removed.

Mistakes That Cost Homeowners Their Carpet

The most common mistake is waiting. Another is walking on soaked carpet, which pushes water deeper and spreads dirt. A third is trying to dry it with heat, which can shrink the backing and cook odors into the fibers.

People also forget the room under the carpet. A wet subfloor gets covered again and traps moisture, so insist on readings before anything new goes down.

  • Do not run a household vacuum on a wet carpet
  • Do not set heaters close to the floor
  • Do not lay new pad on a subfloor that has not been measured

Standards and Sources Behind This Page

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration groups water into clean, gray and black categories. Carpet that was exposed to clean water can often be dried in place, while carpet exposed to gray or black water is typically removed. The Institute also publishes a separate carpet cleaning standard, which is outside the scope of this page.
