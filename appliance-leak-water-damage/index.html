What to Do First When an Appliance Leaks

Water from a dishwasher, washing machine or refrigerator line seeps under cabinets, behind toe kicks and into the subfloor. Because it runs slowly, it can soak a floor for weeks.

First moves:

  • Shut the supply valve behind or beside the appliance
  • Unplug the unit only if the area around it is dry
  • Pull the appliance out if it can be done safely
  • Take photos of the leak, hose and surrounding floor

What fails in household appliances

Washing machine fill hoses burst under pressure, drain hoses slip, and drain pans overflow. Dishwashers leak at door gaskets and inlet fittings. Refrigerator ice maker lines crack or loosen behind the unit.

Water usually spreads under flooring before it shows at the edge, so hardwood, laminate and vinyl plank are common victims.

Signs of a hidden appliance leak

Pay attention to small changes near appliances.

A soft or spongy floor

Subfloor that gives underfoot is probably wet.

Cabinet base swelling

Particleboard cabinet bottoms swell quickly and do not recover.

A musty smell near the unit

Odors point to damp materials behind the appliance.

Cupped or buckled flooring

Boards that lift at the edges are absorbing water from below.

How we handle an appliance leak

The goal is finding every wet spot before sealing anything.

  1. Locate all moisture

    Meters and a thermal camera map the floor, cabinet base and wall.

  2. Remove standing water

    Extractors pull water from flooring and carpet.

  3. Open access points

    Toe kicks and a section of base are removed so air can reach the cavity.

  4. Dry and recheck

    Dehumidification runs until readings drop to normal.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration describes three water categories.
