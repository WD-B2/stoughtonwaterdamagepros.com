Water damage has dozens of causes, and the right response depends on where the water came from and how long it sat. This list groups our services by cause so you can find the page that fits. If you are unsure, call the line and a dispatcher will help you decide.

Each page explains the signs to look for, how the work is done, what drives the cost and how insurance usually treats that kind of loss. None of them quote exact prices, because price depends on the size and condition of the job, and we explain it before work starts.

How to Choose When You Are Not Sure

Start with the source. If water is running, shut it off and read the pipe, appliance or weather pages that match. If it has already stopped, read the material pages for carpet, hardwood floors or walls.

What you seeStart withWhy
Water coming from a pipeBurst pipe or frozen pipeStop the source, then remove water
Standing water on a floorWater extractionBulk removal comes first
Heavy rain or river waterFlood damageFloodwater is treated as contaminated
A stain on the ceilingCeiling water damageTrapped water above
Musty smell, nothing visibleMold remediationOdor often points to hidden moisture

If you are unsure which row fits, call and describe what you see. The dispatcher will ask a few plain questions and send you to the right service.

What Every Service Page Covers

Each page follows a similar structure so you can compare. It describes the cause, the signs to look for, the process, the options and trade-offs, the factors that shape cost and how insurance usually treats that type of loss.

The emergency pages also state what to do while you wait for the crew. Pages about slower problems, such as mold or hardwood floors, explain how a scheduled visit works.

Services and the Order Work Happens

Most jobs touch several pages. A burst pipe starts with source control, moves to extraction and ends with drying. A basement flood starts with pump-out, moves to cleaning and ends with drying and sometimes mold prevention. Read the pages in the order your situation unfolds.

Standards and Sources Behind This Page

Process descriptions follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration and the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for professional mold remediation. Insurance points follow guidance from the Massachusetts Division of Insurance.
