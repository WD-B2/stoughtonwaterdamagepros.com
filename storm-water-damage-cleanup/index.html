Stay away from downed wires, avoid standing water near panels, and place buckets under roof leaks. Do not climb onto a wet or icy roof.

We focus on interior water and drying. Roofers and tree services handle the exterior.

Storm water gets in fast and spreads wide

Wind-driven rain enters through windows, roof edges and flashing. Runoff pushes against foundation walls. In winter, ice dams force meltwater under shingles and into walls and ceilings.

While the storm continues:

  • Move valuables away from leaks
  • Use tarps or buckets to limit spread
  • Keep away from standing water and cords
  • Photograph damage for your insurer

Weather patterns that hit Stoughton

Massachusetts has had repeated federal winter storm declarations, including January 2015 (DR-4214), March 2018 (DR-4372 and DR-4379) and January 2022 (DR-4651). Stoughton's own record includes flooding on March 29, 2010, 1.90 inches of rain reported on July 6, 2018 and a flash flood on June 28, 2020.

Different storms do different damage. Wind and rain stress roofs, snow weight stresses framing, and intense rain stresses drains.

Signs of storm water intrusion

Check these after any major weather.

Wet attic insulation

Water at the roof deck soaks insulation first.

Stains near windows

Wind-driven rain leaks past old seals.

Ceiling drips at edges

Ice dams push water past eaves into walls.

Basement water after rain

Runoff and groundwater find low points.

How we respond after a storm

We work interior-first and coordinate with exterior trades.

  1. Make safe

    The crew checks hazards and identifies the entry points.

  2. Remove water

    Pumps, extractors and wet vacs clear the interior.

  3. Open and dry

    Wet insulation, drywall and trim are removed where needed.

  4. Document

    Photos and moisture logs support your claim.

What affects cost

What to Do Once the Rain Stops

Walk the house with a flashlight and look at ceilings, window corners and the attic hatch if it is safe. Photograph every wet spot and write down the time. Lift furniture off wet carpet and set foil under the legs.

Then call. Storm response is first-come and priority-ranked, and early photos let us plan equipment before we arrive.
