How a Restoration Visit Works

The order is the same on almost every job. It keeps work from being skipped when everyone is under pressure.

  1. Stop and assess

    The crew confirms the source is off and maps the wet areas with meters and a thermal camera.

  2. Extract water

    Pumps and extractors remove standing water from floors and carpets.

  3. Dry the structure

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run, and readings are checked daily.

  4. Document and hand off

    You receive photos and readings for your insurer, and repairs can begin on dry framing.

What to Do Before the Crew Arrives

These steps help in almost every water emergency, and none needs special tools.

  • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main valve
  • Stay out of standing water near outlets and the panel
  • Lift furniture, rugs and electronics off wet floors
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet floors

What Stoughton Water Looks Like

Stoughton's local hazard plan lists repeat flooding at the Red Wing Brook area, Pratts Court, the West Street culvert near Ames Long Pond and a storm drain behind the Police Station. Steep Hill Brook drains about 5.3 square miles, and roughly 1,389 acres of town fall in a FEMA special flood hazard area.

The town has seen flooding on March 29, 2010, rainfall on July 6, 2018 and a flash flood on June 28, 2020. The plan's dam table lists four dams, two rated low hazard and two non-jurisdictional, and water comes from seven wells, six along the western boundary and one near Sumner Street. The historic station on Wyman Street, built in 1888, anchors a downtown where Route 138 carries retail traffic and Route 24 has three interchanges in town.

Does insurance cover this?

Standard Massachusetts homeowners policies do not cover flood or basement seepage, and sewer backup or sump pump failure usually needs an endorsement. Sudden and accidental pipe and appliance failures are often covered. Report the loss promptly and keep your photos.
