The Neighborhood Around Woods Pond

Woods Pond sits close to the center of Stoughton, and a Homes.com town guide says people fish, canoe and hike around it. Real estate listings also use the name The Trails of Woods Pond for a residential area.

We describe this area by its landmark rather than by a boundary, because no source we found draws one. If you live on a street that runs near the pond, call and give your address.

What the Hazard Plan Lists

The Norfolk County flood study summarized in the 2024 Abington, Easton and Stoughton Hazard Mitigation Plan names Woods Pond among the town's potential flooding sources. The plan's dam table lists Woods Pond Dam as low hazard potential and in poor condition.

A low hazard rating describes what a failure could affect, and it is not a forecast. These are plan facts about the pond, not a record of flooding at any home. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, and remember that a low-risk zone does not mean flooding cannot happen.

Rain, Runoff and Sump Pumps

Homes near a pond or brook often rely on a sump pump after heavy rain, and a pump that fails or loses power can let water collect on the floor. A power outage during a storm is a common reason.

If water is rising, do not go down to check it when the panel or outlets are near the water. Call the line and read our storm water damage cleanup page for what to do while you wait.

Clean Water or Not

Water from a burst pipe is usually clean at first, but water that enters from outside is a different matter. Runoff can carry soil, fuel or sewage, and it should be treated as contaminated until a professional says otherwise.

Our flood damage cleanup page explains how contaminated water changes what can be saved.

While You Wait

Keep people out

Keep children and pets away from standing water, especially in a basement.

Power down safely

Only switch off power if you can reach the panel without stepping into water. Otherwise call the utility or emergency services.

Lift valuables

Move electronics, documents and keepsakes up from low shelves and the floor.

Capture the damage

Photograph the water line, the walls and the stored items from a safe spot.

Extraction, Drying and Odor

  1. Remove the water

    Pumps and extractors clear standing water so drying can begin.

  2. Check the materials

    Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall are tested for moisture and contamination.

  3. Dry with equipment

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach normal levels.

  4. Watch for mold

    Moisture left behind can support mold, which is why readings matter. See our mold remediation page.

Documenting for Insurance

Flood and basement seepage are usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy, and a separate flood policy covers them. Sudden pipe failures are often treated differently.

Photos, moisture readings and the written scope help describe the work, but we cannot promise any claim outcome.
