    Staying Safe Near Brockton's Flood Zones

    If you live on or near one of the streets named in the Trout Brook study, plan ahead.     Multi-Unit Buildings and Shared Walls

    In buildings with several apartments, a leak in one unit can reach another through shared walls and floors. Tell neighbors and the landlord at once, shut off the unit's supply and document everything. Our crews can dry the affected unit and advise on the common areas.

    Emergency Water Removal in Brockton

    Emergency water removal in Brockton often begins with a flooded basement or a burst pipe. The city's 2021 Trout Brook flood study maps a 6.97 square mile watershed through the CSX railyard between Elliot and Court Streets.

    Flood-affected areas listed in the study include the Ames Street area, Tukis Park, East Ashland Street, Puffer Playground, Teele Street, Riverside Street, Court Street, Plymouth Street, Peckham Avenue, North Manchester Street, Snow Park and the Moody Street neighborhood.

    What the Trout Brook Study Says About Risk

    The Court Street culvert, 19.2 feet wide, is overtopped by about 1.5 feet in the 100-year event. About 15 structures have lowest floors within 3 feet of the 100-year level, and buildings in the railyard begin flooding in the 2-year storm.

    Owners in those areas should read flood damage restoration and ask their insurer about flood coverage, which standard policies exclude.
