Hardwood floor water damage repair in Stoughton starts with drying, not sanding. Call us to plan a visit. Remove standing water, lift rugs, wipe the surface and keep the floor ventilated. Do not apply heat or force-dry with high heat, which can crack boards.

Wood expands when wet and shrinks when dry. How fast it dries decides whether it recovers.

How floors get wet

Appliance leaks, burst pipes, plumbing under the floor and wet basements all send water into hardwood from above or below. Water from below is harder to notice because the boards cup before they stain.

Engineered hardwood has a plywood core that reacts differently than solid planks.

Signs of water damage in hardwood

Look at the board edges and finish.

Cupping

Edges rise higher than the center when moisture comes from below.

Crowning

The center rises after cupped boards are sanded too early.

Buckling

Boards lift off the subfloor, a sign of severe moisture.

Black or gray stains

Dark staining suggests long-term moisture.

How we dry hardwood

Patience protects the boards.

  1. Measure moisture

    Meters check boards and subfloor against a dry reference.

  2. Extract and ventilate

    Extractors and air movers remove surface water.

  3. Use drying mats

    Mats pull moisture from beneath the boards.

  4. Reassess

    After drying, we review what can be sanded or replaced.

What to Expect When We Visit

A wood floor assessment starts with a moisture meter set for wood, plus readings in the subfloor where access allows. The crew checks the finish, board edges and any gaps, then compares the readings to a dry reference board from the same room.

You will hear whether the floor looks like a drying job, a spot repair or a replacement, along with what could change that verdict once the boards are dry.

Mistakes That Ruin Wet Wood Floors

Heat is the biggest. Heaters, hair dryers and direct sun dry the surface faster than the core, so boards crack and gap. Sanding too early is next, since boards that look flat while still damp will shrink and leave ridges later.

Covering the floor with plastic or rugs is another mistake. Trapped moisture lingers, and stains deepen.

  • Lift rugs and mats right away
  • Let boards dry gradually with airflow and dehumidification
  • Wait for the all-clear before sanding or refinishing

What to Do Before We Arrive

These steps give wood the best chance.

  • Remove standing water and lift rugs and mats
  • Wipe the surface and let air move across it
  • Do not use heaters, hair dryers or high heat
  • Do not sand, seal or cover the floor until it is dry

Standards and Sources Behind This Page

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration treats wood as a material that releases moisture slowly and recommends measuring it against a dry reference. That is why this page asks for readings before any sanding or refinishing decision.
